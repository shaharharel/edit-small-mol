\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Hit-to-lead (H2L) is structurally and operationally distinct from de novo drug discovery. While de novo discovery searches an unbounded chemical space for any molecule that binds a given target, H2L starts from a validated ligand and asks the narrower question: how do we optimize \emph{this} chemotype into a viable clinical candidate. Medicinal chemists begin from a measured hit with a known binding mode and traverse a small number of structure-activity-relationship (SAR) steps toward a candidate that is both potent and developable. Each generated molecule is judged on many axes (predicted affinity, drug-likeness, synthetic tractability, selectivity, metabolic stability, physicochemical fit to the binding-site environment, and absence of toxicophores). For \emph{covalent} programs an additional, hard constraint applies: the candidate must retain a defined electrophilic warhead positioned for the canonical thia-Michael transition state \cite{lonsdale2018structure,burgi1974stereochemistry}. A molecule that loses its warhead is no longer a covalent inhibitor, and a warhead presented in the wrong orientation may either fail to reach the intended cysteine or react promiscuously elsewhere on the proteome. The covalent generative problem is therefore not merely ``generate a potent ligand'' but ``generate a potent ligand whose specific electrophilic atom can reach the target nucleophile in the productive transition-state geometry''.

The covalent-aware generative design problem can be decomposed into a short stack of progressively harder constraints, C1 through C4, each adding one requirement to the row above:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{C1 - Valid drug-like ligand.} Any pretrained chemical language model (e.g.\ the mol2mol prior \cite{loeffler2024reinvent4}) addresses this. Required signal: ligand sequence.
  \item \textbf{C2 - Warhead substructure retained.} Addressed either by fine-tuning on a warhead-biased dataset (\Cvcovaft{}) or by hard-freezing the warhead atoms at the SMILES level (LibInvent \cite{fialkova2022libinvent} and similar scaffold-decoration models). Required signal: ligand sequence.
  \item \textbf{C3 - Warhead in planar productive conformation.} The warhead must adopt the s-cis / s-trans enone geometry needed for $\pi$-conjugation, which is the prerequisite for the alkene to be electrophilic at all. Hard-template-freezing reaches C3 by extending the frozen fragment to include the atoms that lock the dihedral, at the cost of generation flexibility (more atoms frozen $\Rightarrow$ less novel chemistry). Required signal: ligand sequence + 3D conformer.
  \item \textbf{C4 - Warhead reaches the target nucleophile in the pocket.} The warhead's $\beta$-carbon must physically reach the target cysteine in the productive transition-state orientation. A planar warhead in the wrong corner of the pocket is electronically productive but physically unreachable, so C3 does not imply C4. Required signal: ligand sequence + pocket + 3D pose.
\end{itemize}

Existing generative-chemistry methods address subsets of this stack but do not jointly enforce all four. SMILES sequence generators, including the mol2mol prior used in this work \cite{loeffler2024reinvent4}, the original REINFORCE-style chemical RL of Olivecrona et al.\ \cite{olivecrona2017reinvent}, and substructure-constrained de novo generators \cite{blaschke2020reinvent2}, reach C1--C2 and can be steered with substructure filters but are pose-agnostic. Scaffold-decoration tools such as LibInvent \cite{fialkova2022libinvent} reach C3 by template-freezing the warhead atoms, but at the cost of restricting generation to a fixed parent scaffold. Pocket-aware 3D-generative methods such as DiffSBDD \cite{schneuing2024diffsbdd}, Pocket2Mol \cite{peng2022pocket2mol}, and TargetDiff \cite{guan2023targetdiff} produce ligand atoms and coordinates inside the binding cavity (analogous to a non-covalent C4) but face a separate difficulty: converting the predicted atom positions back to a SMILES requires post-hoc bond perception from coordinates, which is heuristic and lossy, and frequently produces invalid or chemically implausible molecules. None of these methods rewards an electrophilic warhead, let alone its productive orientation. Structure-prediction cofold methods \cite{passaro2025boltz2} can validate poses at C4 quality but are too slow to drive an online generative policy. No single existing tool starts from a measured hit and jointly achieves C2--C4.

\covagen{} bridges this gap. It is a hit-to-lead framework built on a \emph{3D-covalent-aware architecture and fine-tuning} of a SMILES language model: a covalent-adapted SMILES decoder is extended with a pocket and warhead-pose conditioning module so that the decoder can generate molecules whose electrophilic atom is presented in the geometry the target nucleophile requires. The policy is driven by a multi-objective RL loop that jointly rewards predicted potency, warhead retention, and drug-likeness.

The contributions of this work are as follows.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{A geometry-conditioned covalent molecular generator.} We introduce \Cvcond{}, a SMILES decoder conditioned on both the target pocket and the desired warhead pose, allowing generation to account for the distance, approach angle, and planarity required for productive covalent binding.
  \item \textbf{A covalent hit-to-lead optimization framework.} Anchoring on a specific starting molecule, we combine \covagen{}-v2 with multi-objective policy optimization that jointly steers generation toward candidates satisfying three axes important to hit-to-lead (predicted potency, warhead retention, and drug-likeness).
  \item (SOON) Prospective wet-lab validation of selected candidates.
\end{enumerate}
