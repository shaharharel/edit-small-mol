\subsection*{Overview of experiments}
\label{sec:results:overview}

We organize the results by the two families of methods. \textbf{\covagen{} v1} establishes the SMILES-only baseline: covalent fine-tuning of the mol2mol prior concentrates electrophilic kinase chemistry (\S\ref{sec:results:covft}), and a three-component DAP RL loop on top of the prior optimizes for predicted potency, warhead retention, and drug-likeness (\S\ref{sec:results:rl_value}). \textbf{\covagen{} v2} adds a geometric channel: pocket and warhead-pose conditioning at the decoder, and we evaluate its effect on generated warhead geometry (\S\ref{sec:results:m1a}); on top of \Cvcond{} we then run DAP RL for potency, warhead retention, and drug-likeness to co-optimize the hit-to-lead axes with the geometry gain (\S\ref{sec:results:covdpo}). Throughout the section we score every cohort on a shared panel of metrics spanning SMILES chemistry, drug-likeness, anchor similarity, warhead chemistry, intrinsic warhead geometry, and pocket-context binding, summarised in Table~\ref{tab:metric_panel}.

\subsection{Covalent fine-tuning concentrates electrophilic kinase chemistry}
\label{sec:results:covft}

We first test whether covalent fine-tuning of the mol2mol prior meaningfully biases the generative distribution toward covalent-inhibitor chemistry. We compare two generative approaches, both seeded with Mol1: (i) \textbf{base}, the vanilla mol2mol prior with no covalent fine-tuning, and (ii) \textbf{\Cvcovaft{}}, fine-tuned on CovInDB v2 ($\sim$9.5k covalent kinase-inhibitor SMILES). From each prior we sampled 10k Mol1-seeded molecules and computed per-cohort metrics.

Results are summarized in Table~\ref{tab:covft}. The dominant shift is warhead retention: \Cvcovaft{} raises acrylamide retention from $0.4\%$ (base) to $94.9\%$, reflecting the covalent-inhibitor bias of the CovInDB v2 fine-tuning corpus in which $\sim 24\%$ of the $\sim 8{,}300$ deduplicated SMILES carry the parent acrylamide and $\sim 48\%$ carry a Michael-acceptor warhead. Other panel metrics remain reasonable: validity $\sim 99\%$, scaffold uniqueness $\sim 9\%$, QED $\sim 0.73$, and median Tc-to-Mol1 near the SAR-hop centre ($0.64$), indicating no mode collapse and that the \Cvcovaft{} prior stays chemically close to Mol1. \Cvcovaft{} also produces $5\times$ more \emph{novel-hinge} molecules ($0.9\% \to 4.7\%$) (molecules carrying a kinase hinge-binding pharmacophore distinct from Mol1's 4-aminoimidazole) confirming the fine-tuning step is more than a SMARTS gate on the warhead and explores kinase-relevant chemistry beyond the seed.

\subsection{RL adds value over the covalent prior alone}
\label{sec:results:rl_value}

The \Cvcovaft{} prior of \S\ref{sec:results:covft} already concentrates kinase covalent chemistry; here we ask whether the multi-objective DAP RL loop adds value over sampling from that policy directly. We compare two cohorts seeded with Mol1: (i) the \textbf{\Cvcovaft{}} cohort, 10k samples drawn directly from the covalent fine-tuned policy with no RL; and (ii) the \textbf{\Cvdap{}} cohort, 10k samples drawn after $50$ DAP steps with the three-component composite reward.

Scaffold uniqueness triples ($9.3\% \to 23.3\%$) and top-scaffold share halves ($26.1\% \to 13.3\%$), indicating the policy diversifies within the acrylamide chassis rather than collapsing to a single mode.

\paragraph{Reward-component ablation.}\label{sec:results:ablation} Table~\ref{tab:rl_value}'s QED drop ($0.73 \to 0.62$) raises a question: is QED doing useful work in the reward, or would we lose it entirely without the term? Table~\ref{tab:ablation} runs three single-component drop-outs (one reward term removed at a time; the remaining two renormalize). Removing QED costs $-0.16$ QED (its own metric) \emph{and} $-0.28$ FiLM-pIC$_{50}$: without a drug-likeness anchor the policy drifts out of ChEMBL-like space where FiLM was trained, hurting both axes. FiLM remains the dominant potency driver in isolation ($-0.55$ pIC$_{50}$) and SMARTS is the warhead lock (warhead $\to 0.5\%$), but at only a $0.10$ weight QED is keeping the policy inside the chemistry region where the pIC$_{50}$ predictor is reliable.

\subsection{Geometry-aware generation via \Cvcond{} recovers the warhead-fixed geometric ceiling}
\label{sec:results:m1a}

We previously showed that \Cvcovaft{} produces warhead-bearing molecules and \Cvdap{} raises their predicted potency, but neither shapes the warhead's 3D geometry: planar-dihedral median stays at $\sim 62^\circ$ (Tables~\ref{tab:covft} and \ref{tab:rl_value}), well outside the $\pm 20^\circ$ window in which the vinyl-amide is $\pi$-conjugated and thus actually electrophilic, so the warhead is present but not electronically primed to react as a Michael acceptor. Here we ask whether the pocket and warhead-pose conditioning of \Cvcond{} (\S\ref{sec:methods:m1a}) addresses that gap. We compare three cohorts of 10k Mol1-anchored samples each: \textbf{\Cvcovaft{}}, \textbf{\Cvdap{}}, and \textbf{\Cvcond{}} (pocket + Mol1 warhead-pose conditioning at inference).

\begin{table}[!t]
\centering
\caption{\Cvcovaft{} vs \Cvdap{} vs \Cvcond{}, $n{=}10\text{k}$ Mol1-anchored per column.}
\label{tab:m1a_geometry}
\small
\setlength{\tabcolsep}{6pt}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.15}
\begin{tabular}{lrrr}
\toprule
                                            & \textbf{v1-covaFT}   & \textbf{v1-DAP}   & \textbf{v2-cond}   \\
\midrule
Validity                                    & 98.9\%           & 98.4\%            & 76.4\%                 \\
Scaffold uniqueness                         & 9.3\%            & 23.3\%            & 23.8\%                 \\
Top-scaffold share                          & 26.1\%           & 13.3\%            & 38.2\%                 \\
\textbf{QED}$^{*}$                          & 0.73             & 0.62              & $\mathbf{0.86}$        \\
Predicted pIC$_{50}$ frac $\ge 7.0$         & 6.9\%            & 13.8\%            & 9.3\%                  \\
Median Tc to Mol1                           & 0.64             & 0.59              & 0.64                   \\
Acrylamide retention                         & 94.9\%           & 99.3\%            & 94.2\%                 \\
\textbf{Pre-reactivity (frac $\ge 0.5$)}$^{*}$ & 0.48             & 0.41              & $\mathbf{0.60}$        \\
\textbf{Planar-dihedral median (deg)}$^{***}$ & $61.8^\circ$     & $62.9^\circ$      & $\mathbf{2.6^\circ}$   \\

\bottomrule
\end{tabular}

Table~\ref{tab:m1a_geometry} presents the three-cohort comparison. \Cvcond{} brings the ligand-only planar-dihedral median down from $\sim 62^\circ$ (both \Cvcovaft{} and \Cvdap{}) to $\sim 3^\circ$, a $\sim 20\times$ improvement in intrinsic warhead conformation. A plausible explanation is the combination of two ingredients: at inference we condition on Mol1's own warhead pose (approach-angle $\theta_{BD}\!\approx\!105^\circ$, planar dihedral $\phi_{\text{planar}}\!\approx\!0^\circ$, S$_\gamma$--C$_\beta$ distance $\approx 1.85$~\r{A}), and the covalent-adapted training set (covalent-inhibitor SMILES paired with their PDB co-crystal pose in a pocket) supplies examples where the ligand's warhead sits at exactly that productive geometry. The resulting policy appears to generate SMILES whose intrinsic ETKDG conformer aligns with the conditioning target.

The geometry gains extend beyond the ligand-only planar dihedral to the pocket-context axes as well: pre-reactivity fraction rises to $0.60$ (vs $0.48$ / $0.41$; more molecules keep the warhead near the reactive planar face), and covalent-Vina median drops to $126$ kcal/mol (vs $147$ / $183$), reflecting a better docked pose to Cys346. A potential explanation for this is that the covalent-adapted training set consists of PDB co-crystals in which real covalent inhibitors sit in their productive binding pose, so a model that reproduces that chemistry may inherit the docking energetics that come with it. On top of the geometric axes, QED rises to $0.86$ (vs $0.73$ / $0.62$), the one drug-likeness axis that was not directly encoded in the supervision, potentially improving as a byproduct of the shift toward the drug-like real-world crystal-binder distribution.

\subsection{Adding potency, drug-likeness, and cofold-quality preferences on top of \Cvcond{}}
\label{sec:results:covdpo}

We previously showed that \Cvcond{} addresses the warhead-geometry gap architecturally (\S\ref{sec:results:m1a}). Here we ask whether we can further improve predicted potency, drug-likeness, and pocket-context binding on top of the v2 policy. We compare two cohorts of 10k Mol1-anchored samples each: (i) \textbf{\Cvcond{}}, the v2 pocket + pose-conditioned policy of \S\ref{sec:results:m1a}, sampled directly with no RL; and (ii) \textbf{\Cvcond{}+DAP}, adding the three-component DAP reward (predicted pIC$_{50}$, warhead SMARTS, QED) of \S\ref{sec:methods:rl} on top of \Cvcond{}.
